# Universal Analysis Techniques

Detail file. Load only when a technique from this category is selected in PHASE 3.

These techniques are applicable to any type of idea—creative, product, or service.

## Devil's Advocate Analysis

**When to use:** Always relevant. Identifies potential failures and weaknesses.

**Approach:**

- What could go wrong with this idea?
- What assumptions are weak or unproven?
- Why might this idea NOT work?
- What obstacles will be encountered?
- What counterarguments exist?

**Output structure:**

- Major concerns (ranked by severity)
- Weak assumptions identified
- Potential failure modes
- Obstacles and challenges

## Assumption Testing

**When to use:** When the idea relies on unvalidated beliefs about users, technology, or market.

**Approach:**

- What assumptions underpin this idea?
- Which assumptions are critical vs nice-to-have?
- How can each assumption be tested?
- What happens if key assumptions are wrong?

**Output structure:**

- List of core assumptions
- Risk level per assumption (high/medium/low)
- Testing methods for validation
- Contingency plans if assumptions fail

## Alternative Approaches

**When to use:** To explore different paths to the same goal.

**Approach:**

- What other ways exist to solve this problem?
- What are simpler/cheaper/faster alternatives?
- What trade-offs does each approach have?
- Why is the proposed idea better than alternatives?

**Output structure:**

- 3-5 alternative approaches
- Comparison table (complexity, cost, time, effectiveness)
- Trade-off analysis
- Justification for original approach or pivot recommendation

## Ethical Implications

**When to use:** When the idea affects people, society, or raises moral questions.

**Approach:**

- Who could be harmed by this idea?
- What are potential misuse cases?
- Are there privacy, fairness, or bias concerns?
- What unintended consequences might arise?

**Output structure:**

- Potential harms identified
- Vulnerable groups affected
- Mitigation strategies
- Ethical boundaries to establish

## Pre-mortem Analysis

**When to use:** To surface risks that argument-style critique misses — imagine the project has already failed and reconstruct why.

**Approach:**

- It is 12 months from now and the project has failed completely — tell the story of what went wrong
- What was the single most likely cause of death? (be specific, not generic)
- What early warning signs were ignored along the way?
- Which failure causes are within your control, and which are external?
- What would you change today to prevent the most likely failure story?

**Output structure:**

- Failure narrative(s) — the most plausible paths to failure
- Ranked failure causes (likelihood × impact)
- Early warning signs to watch for
- Preventive changes to make now

## MVP/Scope Reduction

**When to use:** To find the smallest version that tests the core hypothesis — especially before moving to /project-plan.

**Approach:**

- What is the core hypothesis this idea stands or falls on?
- What is the absolute smallest version that tests that hypothesis?
- Which elements can be cut without killing the core? Which feel essential but aren't?
- What is in the concept because it's exciting rather than because it's necessary?
- How would the concept be phased: core first, then what, in which order?

**Output structure:**

- Core hypothesis stated explicitly
- Minimum viable version defined
- Cut list (deferred elements with rationale)
- Phased scope recommendation (v0 → v1 → later)
